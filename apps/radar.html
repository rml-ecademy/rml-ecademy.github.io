DISPLAY MODES & COLLISION AVOIDANCE LAB

EXERCISE: own ship heads 000° at 12 kn STW; T01 heads 180° at 12 kn through water, initially 8 NM north. Both experience the same current. T02 is anchored. Ideal sensors, uniform current, no leeway; instantaneous vectors for teaching.

  1. Select CROSS CURRENT or NO CURRENT from MENU → SCENARIO, then press OK to apply. Both start paused. Use TRUE vectors and switch SEA / GROUND.
  2. Compare T01's true vector with its through-water direction. Observe the anchored target: zero ground velocity, but a vector opposite the current in sea reference.
  3. Switch to REL vectors. With consistent inputs, changing stabilization alone does not change relative vectors or CPA/TCPA.
  4. Choose COURSE UP, change heading, then compare HEAD UP. Course Up holds its reference until you press RESET C-UP.
  5. Transmit to run the encounter, or reset the scenario to compare the initial values. This is an exercise, not a manoeuvre recommendation.

Orientation, vector reference and motion presentation are separate. This trainer uses centred relative motion (RM) and relative trails, not true-motion display. Target TC*/STW* are estimates in own ship's water reference, not target sensor readings or bow headings. COG/SOG and TC*/STW* stay visible together regardless of vector mode. Use systematic observation, all available information and COLREGs; do not infer heading solely from a ground vector. Sea-reference aspect estimates also depend on accurate inputs and current/leeway assumptions.

Reference: MCA MGN 379 — radar stabilization and electronic navigation. UK guidance prefers sea stabilization for open-sea anti-collision decisions. Unofficial simplified training model.

Why does this happen? · Formula and model limits

Radar observes relative motion. For this exercise: estimated target water velocity = heading-biased relative velocity + reported own water velocity. A constant heading bias rotates both relative position and relative velocity. Incorrect STW changes the added own-ship vector, not the measured relative motion. A steady rotation preserves CPA/TCPA here. This does NOT show that sensor faults are harmless: transient heading errors, tracking filters, lost targets, trial manoeuvres and equipment-specific processing are not modelled.

Correct-input target estimate assumes shared current and no leeway; target vector is not a measurement of its bow heading. This is a simplified mathematical lesson, not an emulation of a particular FURUNO fault response.

Instructor debrief: Which input is wrong? Which displayed values changed? Why is unchanged CPA not evidence of correct sensors? Cross-check gyro, log, selected sources and observations; do not choose a passing side from a suspect true vector.

Technical reference: MCA MGN 379, sections 3.2–3.4: incorrect heading/speed inputs can corrupt true vectors and interpretation.
